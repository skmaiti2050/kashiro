# Best Man's Toast for Rohan and Aisha

*Draft for the reception at the Taj, Udaipur, on [[14 December 2025]]. Speaker: Kabir (best man and Rohan's friend since Class 6). Target time: 6 to 7 minutes. Notes in brackets are for me, not to be read out.*

---

Good evening, everyone.

[Wait for the room to settle. Smile. Do not start talking while the DJ is still fading the music.]

For those of you who don't know me, my name is Kabir, and I have had the great honour, and occasionally the great misfortune, of being Rohan's best friend for nearly twenty years. We met in Class 6 at a school in Dehradun, when he was the new boy and I was the only person in the class who didn't already have a bench partner. Our teacher, [[Mrs. D'Souza]], put us together, and I don't think she realised what she was starting.

[Pause. Look at Rohan.]

I want to begin by thanking Aisha's parents, [[Mr. and Mrs. Qureshi]], and Rohan's parents, [[Dr. Sanjay Kapoor]] and Mrs. Anita Kapoor, for bringing all of us here to Udaipur. I've been to many weddings, but I've never been to one where the view from the lunch table was a palace in the middle of a lake. {{Its|It's|j}} been an extraordinary few days, and I know a huge amount of work went into making them so smooth. Thank you, from all of us.

[Lead the applause. Wait.]

Now, I've been told by several members of both families that this toast must be short, must be clean, and must not mention the trip to Goa in [[2012]]. I will do my best on {{teh|the|s}} first two.

[Wait for the laugh. If there's no laugh, move on quickly.]

## About Rohan

Let me tell you {{an|a|s}} little about the groom. Rohan is one of {{the the|the|s}} kindest people I have ever known. He is also one of the most stubborn. When we were fifteen, he decided he was going to learn the guitar. He practised every single day for a year. He was terrible for the entire year. He is still, honestly, not very good. But he never stopped, and last month he played a song at the sangeet that made half the room cry. Some of them were crying because it was beautiful.

[Beat.]

That's Rohan. He doesn't give up on the things he loves, even when those things don't love him back right away. Which brings me, of course, to Aisha.

## How they met

Rohan and Aisha met at a friend's birthday party in Bengaluru in [[2019]]. I was there. I remember it clearly, because Rohan spent the first hour telling me he was going to leave early, and the next four hours talking to Aisha on the balcony. When I finally found him at two in the morning and said it was time to go, he looked at me like I {{was|had|j}} asked him to walk into the sea.

The next morning, he called me and said, "Kabir, I think I've met the person I'm going to marry." I told him he {{were|was|j}} being dramatic. He told me to start writing my speech.

[Hold up the speech cards.]

Well, Rohan, I've been writing it for six years. I hope you're satisfied.

## About Aisha

Aisha, I want to say something to you directly. When Rohan first introduced us, I was a little worried. I'd heard you were a doctor, that you'd run two marathons, and that you could speak four languages. I assumed you would be intimidating. And you are. But you are also warm, funny and generous, and you have made my best friend happier than I have ever seen him.

You also taught him to cook, which I didn't think was possible. When we shared a flat in Pune after college, Rohan's entire menu was Maggi and toast. Last month, he made biryani for twelve people, and nobody had to go to hospital. That is your achievement, Aisha, and I hope the {{Indain|Indian|s}} Medical Association recognises it.

[Wait for the laugh.]

## The serious bit

[Slow down here. Don't rush.]

I want to be serious for a moment. A few years ago, Rohan went through a difficult time. His company closed, he lost a lot of money, and for a while he wasn't sure what to do next. I watched Aisha during that time. She never once made him feel like he had failed. She helped him {{rebuilt|rebuild|j}}, quietly and patiently, and today he runs a business that he's proud of.

I think that's what a good marriage {{look|looks|j}} like. Not the photographs or the palace or the fireworks, though those are very nice. It's the person who stays beside you when things are hard, and who believes in you when you've stopped believing in yourself. Rohan, you found that person. Aisha, so did you.

## Advice

I'm told the best man is supposed to give some advice. I'm not married, so I asked the experts. I asked my parents, who have been married for [[38 years]], what the secret was. My father said, "Always agree with your wife." My mother said, "He's never once done that." So I'm not sure that helps.

[Pause for laugh.]

Instead, I'll offer one thing I've learned from watching the two of you. You never keep score. When one of you is tired, the other one cooks. When one of you is having a bad week, the other one plans something silly to cheer them up. You both give more {{then|than|j}} you take, and somehow you both end up with more. I think that's the real secret, and I hope you never lose it.

## The toast

[Pick up the glass. Look around the room.]

So, would everyone please stand and raise {{you|your|j}} glasses.

[Wait until most people are standing.]

To Rohan, who never gives up on anything he loves. To Aisha, who gave him something worth never giving up on. May your home always be full of music, even if some of it is Rohan's guitar. May your arguments always be short and your holidays always be long. And may you always be as happy as you look tonight.

To Rohan and Aisha!

[Drink. Sit down. Do not trip over the microphone cable this time.]
