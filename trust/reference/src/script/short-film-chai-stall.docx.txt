# THE LAST CUP: Short Film Screenplay

**Written by Ishaan Bhatt. Draft 2, [[2 November 2025]]. Running time: approximately 12 minutes.**

## SCENE 1. EXT. RAILWAY STATION, PLATFORM 3, KATNI JUNCTION. DAWN.

*A small-town railway platform before sunrise. Mist hangs over the tracks. A single yellow light flickers above a tea stall with a tin roof. The stall is {{teh|the|s}} only thing awake.*

*RAMESH (60s), thin, in a faded grey sweater, pours milk into a dented steel pan. His movements are slow and exact, the movements of someone who has done this ten thousand times. A transistor radio on the shelf plays old Hindi film songs, very softly.*

*A boy, CHHOTU (12), runs up the platform with a crate of glass cups. He {{are|is|j}} out of breath.*

**CHHOTU:** Kaka, the 5:40 is late again. They said twenty minutes.

**RAMESH:** *(without looking up)* They always say twenty minutes.

*Chhotu sets down the crate and begins wiping the cups with a cloth. Ramesh adds tea leaves, ginger and sugar to the pan. Steam rises.*

**CHHOTU:** Sharma uncle said the new café will open next week. Inside the station. With chairs.

*Ramesh pauses for half a second. Then he keeps stirring.*

**RAMESH:** Chairs are good. People {{likes|like|j}} chairs.

**CHHOTU:** He said they'll sell coffee in paper cups. Fifty rupees.

**RAMESH:** Fifty rupees for paper. *(He shakes his head.)* Bring me {{the the|the|s}} cardamom.

## SCENE 2. EXT. PLATFORM 3. CONTINUOUS.

*The 5:40 pulls in, twenty-three minutes late. Doors open. A small crowd of passengers spills out, sleepy and cold. Several {{drifts|drift|j}} toward the stall.*

*Ramesh pours tea from a height, a long brown arc into each glass, never spilling a drop. Chhotu hands them out and collects coins. A regular, MR. VERMA (50s), in a sweater vest and a muffler, takes his glass with a nod.*

**MR. VERMA:** Ramesh-bhai. Same as {{everyday|every day|j}}.

**RAMESH:** Same as every day, Verma-ji.

**MR. VERMA:** You heard about the café?

**RAMESH:** I heard.

**MR. VERMA:** *(sipping)* They won't make it like this. Machines don't know how to make tea.

*Ramesh smiles slightly but says nothing. A young woman, PRIYA (20s), with a laptop bag and earphones, looks at the stall uncertainly.*

**PRIYA:** Do you take UPI?

*Chhotu points proudly to a laminated QR code taped to the side of the stall.*

**CHHOTU:** We take everything, didi.

*Priya scans, pays, and takes a glass. She sips, then pauses, surprised.*

**PRIYA:** This is really good.

**RAMESH:** *(quietly)* Forty-one years.

*Priya doesn't hear him. She is already walking away, typing on her phone.*

## SCENE 3. INT. RAMESH'S ROOM, RAILWAY QUARTERS. NIGHT.

*{{An|A|s}} small, neat room. A single bed, a steel trunk, a calendar from [[1998]] still on the wall. A framed photograph of a woman in a green sari. Ramesh sits on the bed, counting the day's money into small piles.*

*Chhotu sits on the floor, doing his homework by the light of a single bulb.*

**CHHOTU:** How much?

**RAMESH:** Six hundred and forty.

**CHHOTU:** That's good, na?

**RAMESH:** It's good.

*He puts the money into a tin box and looks at the photograph for a long moment.*

**CHHOTU:** Kaka, if the café opens, what will we do?

**RAMESH:** We will make tea.

**CHHOTU:** But if nobody comes?

*Ramesh doesn't answer for a while. He reaches over and corrects a sum in Chhotu's notebook with his finger.*

**RAMESH:** Seven times eight is fifty-six. Not fifty-eight.

*Chhotu fixes it. Ramesh lies back on the bed and closes his eyes.*

## SCENE 4. INT. NEW STATION CAFÉ. DAY.

*Bright lights. Glass counters. A coffee machine hissing. Plastic chairs in orange and white. A banner reads "GRAND OPENING". The café is busy with passengers taking photographs.*

*Chhotu stands outside the glass door, peering in. A security guard waves him away.*

## SCENE 5. EXT. PLATFORM 3. DAY.

*The tea stall. Only two customers{{  | |s}}today. Ramesh is wiping the counter that is already clean. The radio plays a sad song from an old film.*

*Mr. Verma arrives, holding a paper cup from the café. He looks embarrassed.*

**MR. VERMA:** My daughter gave me a card. For the café. Free coffee for a month. *(He holds up the cup.)* It's not the same.

**RAMESH:** Drink it. It's free.

**MR. VERMA:** It's not tea.

*He puts the paper cup down on the counter. Ramesh looks at it, then pours a glass of tea and slides it across. Mr. Verma reaches for his wallet. Ramesh waves it away.*

**RAMESH:** Today, {{its|it's|j}} free here too.

*They both laugh, quietly.*

## SCENE 6. EXT. PLATFORM 3. NIGHT. RAIN.

*Heavy rain. The platform is nearly empty. The café's lights glow through the rain at the other end of the station.*

*Ramesh is closing the stall early. Chhotu runs up, soaked, holding his phone.*

**CHHOTU:** Kaka! Kaka, look!

*He shows Ramesh the phone. On the screen: a short video of Ramesh pouring tea from a height, filmed from a few metres away. The caption reads: "Best chai in Madhya Pradesh. 41 years at Katni Junction." The view count is [[2,84,000]].*

**RAMESH:** What is this?

**CHHOTU:** The didi from the train. The one with the laptop. She made a video. Everyone is sharing it!

*Ramesh stares at the screen. He doesn't fully understand. But he watches himself pouring tea, the brown arc catching the yellow light, and something in his face softens.*

## SCENE 7. EXT. PLATFORM 3. DAWN. ONE WEEK LATER.

*The same mist, the same flickering light. But now there is a line at the tea stall: twenty people, maybe more. Some are passengers. Some have clearly come just for the tea. A young couple takes a selfie with Ramesh in {{teh|the|s}} background.*

*Chhotu runs between customers, collecting glasses, laughing. Mr. Verma stands at the front, as always.*

**MR. VERMA:** Ramesh-bhai. Same as every day.

**RAMESH:** Same as every day.

*Ramesh pours. The arc of tea rises and falls. The camera slowly pulls back, past the line of customers, past the empty chairs of the café at the far end of the platform, up into the brightening sky.*

*FADE OUT.*

**TITLE CARD:** *For every small stall that kept a city awake.*
