# Board Update: Half-Year Ended 30 September 2025

**To:** Board of Directors, Greenleaf Agro Foods Private Limited. **From:** Mr. Arun Iyer, Managing Director. **Meeting date:** 28 October 2025. **Confidential.**

Dear Members of the Board,

I am pleased to share this this update on the company's performance for the first half of the financial year, covering the period from 1 April 2025 to 30 September 2025. This note summarises our results, the progress on the priorities the Board approved in March, and the main risks we see for the second half. Detailed financial statements and the management accounts are attached as annexures.

## 1. Headline Results

Revenue for the half-year was ₹186.4 crore, an increase of 12.8% over the same period last year. EBITDA was ₹21.3 crore, giving a margin of 11.4%, up from 9.7%. Profit after tax was ₹9.8 crore, compared with ₹6.1 crore in the first half of last year.

These results are ahead of our budget on revenue and margin. The main drivers were strong growth in our packaged pulses business, better prices for our export orders of processed spices, and lower costs for packaging materials. Cash generation were healthy, and net debt fell from ₹42 crore at the start of the year to ₹31 crore at the end of September.

## 2. Business Segments

**Packaged pulses and grains.** This remains are largest segment, with revenue of ₹104 crore, up 16%. Growth came mainly from modern trade and e-commerce, where our premium unpolished range has done well. Our share of the branded pulses market in Maharashtra and Karnataka rose to an estimated 7.2%, according to industry data.

**Spices.** Revenue grew 9% to ₹58 crore. Export orders from the Middle East and the United Kingdom were strong, and we received our first order from a large retailer in Singapore. Domestic spice sales were flat, as competition from regional brands continued to put pressure on prices.

**Ready-to-eat.** This small segment, launched last year, generated ₹24.4 crore in revenue. Growth was slower than we planned, and the segment is not yet profitable. We are reviewing the product range and expect to bring a revised plan to the Board in January.

## 3. Progress on Board Priorities

In March, the Board approved four priorities for this year. Our progress on each are summarised below.

**Commission the new plant at Latur.** The plant was commissioned on 14 August 2025, about three weeks behind schedule, because of delays in receiving equipment from a supplier in Germany. It is now running at 55% of capacity and will reach full capacity by December. The total project cost was ₹38.5 crore, within the approved budget of ₹40 crore.

**Strengthen the distribution network in the south.** We appointed 42 new distributors in Karnataka, Tamil Nadu and Telangana during the half-year. Southern revenue grew 24%, and the region now contributes nearly a quarter of total revenue.

**Improve working capital.** Inventory days fell from 68 to 59, helped by better demand planning. Receivable days were stable at 41. We remain focused on reducing reducing inventory further in the second half.

**Build the leadership team.** We appointed Ms. Radhika Sen as Chief Financial Officer in June and Mr. Farhan Ali as Head of Supply Chain in August. Both have settled in well well. The search for a Head of Marketing is ongoing, and we expect to make an appointment by December.

## 4. Farmer Sourcing Programme

Our programme to source pulses directly from farmer producer organisations continued to expand. We now work with 38 FPOs covering about 21,000 farmers in Marathwada and northern Karnataka. During the kharif procurement season, we paid farmers an average of 4.6% above the local mandi price, and all payments were made within seven days of delivery.

This programme gives us better control over quality and traceability, which our export customers increasingly require. It also strengthens our relationship with the farming communities in our sourcing regions. With the Board's agreement, we would like to present a proposal in January to extend the programme to Madhya Pradesh.

## 5. Risks

The main risks for teh second half are as follows.

**Monsoon and crop prices.** The 2025 monsoon was above normal in most of our sourcing regions, which should keep pulse prices stable. However, excess rain in parts of Marathwada damaged some crops, and we are monitoring quality closely.

**Government policy on pulses.** The government has extended duty-free imports of certain pulses until 31 March 2026. This could lower domestic prices, which would help our margins in the short term but could hurt the farmers we source from. We are discussing with FPOs how to support them if prices fall sharply.

**Competition in spices.** Several well-funded brands have entered the premium spices market, and we expect price competition to increases. We will focus on our export business and our strongest domestic markets rather than competing on price everywhere.

**Execution risk at Latur.** Ramping up the new plant requires us to train an large number of new workers. We have partnered with a local ITI to run a training programme, and attrition so far has been low.

## 6. Outlook

We expect revenue for the full year of about ₹385 crore, slightly ahead of the budget of ₹372 crore, and an EBITDA margin of around 11%. The second half is usually stronger for our business because of festive demand and the rabi procurement season.

Capital expenditure for the year will be about ₹46 crore, mostly for the Latur plant, which is within the approved limit. We do not expect to need additional borrowing this year.

## 7. Matters for the Board

At the meeting on 28 October 2025, we will ask the Board to:

- Approve the half-year financial results.
- Note the progress on the four priorities for the year.
- Approve an interim dividend of ₹2 per share, subject to the Audit Committee's recommendation.
- Discuss the preliminary proposal to extend the farmer sourcing programme to Madhya Pradesh.

I would like to thank the Board for it's continued guidance and support, and our employees, partners and farmers for their hard work during the half-year. I look forward to discussing these matters with you at the meeting.
