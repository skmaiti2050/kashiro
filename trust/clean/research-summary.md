# Midday meals and attendance: a short summary

This note summarises a survey of 240 government schools in three districts of Odisha, carried out between July 2023 and March 2024 by a team from the state's education department.

## Method

Each school reported its daily attendance for the full year. The team compared months in which a hot meal was served on every school day with months in which the meal was interrupted, for example because of a delay in the supply of rice or dal.

## Findings

1. Attendance was 6.4 percentage points higher in months with an uninterrupted meal.
2. The effect was larger for girls in Classes 6 to 8 than for younger children.
3. Schools that served eggs twice a week saw a further rise of 1.9 points.

The authors note that attendance had had a seasonal pattern long before the scheme began, so they compare each month with the same month in earlier years. They also say that that comparison cannot rule out every other cause, such as a good harvest that keeps older children out of the fields.

## Costs

The meal costs about Rs 9.50 per child per day at the upper primary level, of which the state pays 40 percent. For a school of 300 children, that comes to roughly Rs 7 lakh a year. Across the three districts, the scheme costs about Rs 48 crore annually.

## Limits

The survey covers only government schools, and the attendance registers were not audited. The authors call for a randomised study, and they suggest that headmasters record the reason for each break in the meal, so that later studies can tell a supply problem from a holiday.
